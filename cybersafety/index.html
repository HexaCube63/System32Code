Mastering Network Hygiene & Digital Defense

Your digital footprint is permanent. Understand how attackers operate before you click.


Core Pillars of Cyber Hygiene


01. Passwords & Encryption

Simple passwords can be cracked in milliseconds by modern GPUs. Always use passphrases (14+ characters) and enable end-to-end encryption on messaging and cloud storage.

  • Never reuse the same password across sites
  • Use a dedicated Password Manager
  • Look for HTTPS before entering data
 02. Multi-Factor Auth (MFA)

Even if your password leaks in a data breach, MFA stops attackers from logging in. Avoid SMS codes when possible since SIM-swapping is rising.

  • Prefer Authenticator Apps (TOTP) or Passkeys
  • Store backup recovery codes offline
  • Enable MFA on Email, GitHub, and Banking first
03. Phishing & Social Engineering

Attackers exploit urgency, fear, or fake rewards to trick you into handing over credentials or running malicious files.

  • Inspect the actual sender domain, not just display name
  • Hover over links to preview the real URL
  • Never download unexpected .exe, .zip, or macro docs
 04. Device & Network Defense

Outdated software leaves known security holes wide open. Public Wi-Fi networks also expose unencrypted traffic to snooping.

  • Turn on automatic OS and browser updates
  • Keep your Firewall active at all times
  • Avoid sensitive logins on open public Wi-Fi

Common Attack Vectors


🎣 Spear Phishing

Targeted emails crafted using your public social media info to look like messages from teachers, bosses, or friends.

🦠 Ransomware & Trojans

Malware disguised as free games, cracked software, or PDF invoices that locks your files and demands payment.

📡 Man-in-the-Middle

Fake public hotspots (like "Free_Airport_WiFi") that intercept everything you type while connected.

>_ QUICK DEFENSE AUDIT

Check off the habits you currently follow to verify your cyber readiness:

[1] I use unique passwords for my main accounts.

[2] Two-Factor Authentication (2FA/MFA) is enabled on my email.

[3] My OS and web browser are updated to the latest version.

[4] I verify URLs and senders before clicking links or attachments.

[5] I keep backups of my important code and personal files.
